# Simulator architecture and verification

This reference explains the simulator's mechanisms, general properties and verification contract. It does not certify every physical situation or repeat tuned values. Quantitative settings belong exclusively in `Inputs`, `Fixed` and `Constants` in [model.py](../../model.py). Recorded settings, measured errors and source identities belong in the [verification evidence](MODELING_APPROACH.md#physical-model-verification-gate); remaining work belongs in [NEXT_STEPS.md](../NEXT_STEPS.md).

## Scope and information flow

The airborne simulator couples CAD-derived geometry and mass properties, magnetic calculations, averaged electrical dynamics, MuJoCo rigid-body motion and virtual sensors. It currently models one moving piece over a local coil patch. Contact, whole-board interactions, detailed switching electronics and thermal feedback are separate qualification scopes. Unknown component properties remain modeling assumptions even when their equations are integrated accurately.

The physical state is available to the physics and sensor generators, diagnostics and safety checks. The estimator and controller receive simulated measurements and use their own nominal model; they must not receive the true pose, velocity, physical force matrix or hidden calibration errors. Initialization follows the same rule.

A physical advance works as follows:

- Evaluate the magnetic force/torque map at the actual pose using the CAD-derived windings and permanent magnets. The CAD backend integrates along the curved wire and ideal return using cached Gaussian line elements; the generic polyline backend retains its straight-segment quadrature. Both share force/torque assembly. A verified atlas can replace magnet-field evaluation within its declared domain.
- Use the map and actual linear/angular velocity to calculate motion-induced back-EMF. Advance winding currents under the applied voltages, resistance and inductance. The electrical solution supplies both endpoint currents and interval-average currents.
- Apply the resulting net magnetic force and torque about the piece's centre of mass. MuJoCo advances translation and rotation and supplies gravity and contact, avoiding duplicate forces. CAD mass, centre of mass and inertia remain authoritative; display meshes do not determine them.
- Generate magnetic-field histories from the moving magnets and actual winding currents. Sensor models add their configured filtering, acquisition timing, calibration, noise and digitization. Measurements become available only at their scheduled delivery times.
- At hardware events, update the estimator, controller and current regulator using available readings. Their rates are independent of numerical physics steps.

This is implemented through [motion.py](../motion.py), [airborne.py](../airborne.py), [electrical.py](../electrical.py) and [sensing.py](../sensing.py), reusing [levitation_sim.py](../../levitation_sim.py) for magnetic calculations. Rendering reads recorded motion; changing camera views does not require recalculating physics.

Measured-current coil-field subtraction reconstructs the nominal exponential winding response between current samples and applies the Hall filter and sequential acquisition timing. Its time constant comes from the same nominal resistance and inductance calculation as the driver model. This uses available current readings, not true currents or hidden calibration. Constant drive voltage and slowly varying back-EMF within each sample interval justify the reconstruction; inductance uncertainty, unrecorded switching and disabled-coil extinction require separate qualification.

Commanded local movement uses a smooth rest-to-rest reference with continuous position, velocity and acceleration. Translation is expressed in board axes; orientation follows a fixed world-axis rotation from the initial orientation. The controller uses reference acceleration plus pose and relative-velocity feedback, with angular quantities expressed in world coordinates. The observer still receives only sensor readings and nominal dynamics. Tracking is scored against the reference at the physical timestamp, while the actuation patch remains fixed. Checkpoints identify the motion scenario, and traces retain the target alongside actual and estimated poses. Reference derivatives, rotational frames, target scoring and scenario-preserving restart have dedicated checks; these do not establish successful flight tracking.

There are distinct execution paths. Controlled airborne trials close the sensor-feedback loop. [Physical verification](../physics_verification.py) instead prescribes voltage instructions and initial motion, leaving the piece free to move while recording analogue sensor fields passively. Its persistent-patch handoff adapter retains disabled windings and their decaying currents. Passing that adapter does not establish that position-triggered handoffs are integrated into the controlled-flight path.

Both paths share the airborne height, current, contact and finite-state checks. Height bounds apply to centre-of-mass displacement expressed as an equivalent upright magnet gap; a separate tilted-bottom clearance check prevents contact. Bounds are inclusive and are not widened by a tracking tolerance. The geometry’s nominal gap is the lower bound, so release there with no supporting current can immediately leave the verified range. Controlled trials use the model-defined interior gap fraction for the target and release, with an optional initial disturbance around that reference. Coil identities must remain inside the physical CAD patch, and the local controlled path rejects a change of physical drive window. These guards limit the experiment; they do not establish accuracy throughout a continuous domain.

## Numerical method and its limits

[Adaptive stepping](../adaptive_airborne.py) compares a full advance with successive half advances, recomputing the magnetic map between the half advances. It compares position, orientation, velocities, current and analogue field path/filter response, then accepts the finer branch or restores state and retries a shorter interval. Accepted errors govern later growth. Rejected attempts must not consume noise samples, deliver sensor packets, update the controller or leave field/filter history behind. The handoff adapter also restores its per-winding filter memory.

Scheduled events bound integration intervals. Switching occurs at an event, while current extinction inside an interval uses the electrical model's clipped-current solution and corresponding Hall-filter integral. Failure to achieve local accuracy or encountering an unsupported physical state stops with retained evidence; it must not silently alter limits or substitute a different model.

The current implementation also applies a project-specific mechanical-work budget over the requested duration. This allocation is not a standard global-error estimator. Each physical advance compares the change in kinetic and gravitational energy with magnetic work supplied by the held back-EMF and the integrated winding current. The independently verified winding solution accounts for electrical storage and resistive losses; its power transfer is the mechanical-work reference. The accepted half-steps spend the sum of their absolute defects, so opposite signs cannot cancel. The allowance scales with gravity energy across the permitted flight-height span and is distributed in proportion to interval duration. Rejected branches spend nothing; accepted accounting survives checkpoint restart. This checks accumulated energy consistency, not global position or current error. Contact and unaccounted external work are outside this airborne criterion.

Event clipping changes the interval being attempted, not the nominal step-size sequence. After a retry, the next proposal remains a subdivision of the requested step. This prevents a clipped interval from introducing a persistent timing offset and unnecessary tiny event-remainder steps; hardware schedules and their time-roundoff allowance remain unchanged.

The winding solution is exact for its held voltage/back-EMF assumptions, not for arbitrary motion during a whole interval. The motion-dependent coupling still has integration error. Step comparison estimates local temporal error; it cannot by itself bound accumulated error, find a shared equation/sign mistake, or reveal a magnetic approximation common to both branches. Smaller time steps do not improve conductor geometry or atlas interpolation.

Direct and cached magnetics share material and geometry assumptions. Agreement verifies the cache against that reference, not the magnetic model against reality. Atlas domain/source checks are runtime guards; sampled interpolation checks are not a mathematical bound over every point in the domain. Physical noise must be modeled separately and must not be used to disguise numerical error.

The stationary coil-to-Hall map has its own chord resolution, independent of the force integration order. It is computed at setup and used by both the physical sensor generator and nominal estimator; modeled calibration mismatch remains separate. Physical magnetic refinement increases force quadrature order and shortens Hall chords. Explicit refinement arguments to `HallFieldModel` select absolute conductor resolution for diagnostics. Reports identify both resolutions, and changed settings invalidate old checkpoints and caches. Atlas generation uses the same force-quadrature factory as the physical plant. Unsupported edits to CAD polyline vertices are rejected rather than silently assigning them a different curved path.

## Joint integration candidate

The verified joint method in [joint_airborne.py](../joint_airborne.py) uses SciPy RK45 for the coupled airborne state. Its bounded numerical verification is complete, and it is now used for controlled hover and local movement. Legacy execution paths remain available for their retained evidence. The [candidate evidence](MODELING_APPROACH.md#joint-integration-candidate) records numerical scope, checkpoint verification and remaining exclusions. DOP853 and Radau remain independent reference methods. The [reduced solver comparison](MODELING_APPROACH.md#joint-integration-review) supports this starting choice without measuring full CAD-field performance.

[MuJoCo documents continuous forward dynamics and external integration](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/computation/#numerical-integration). Retain its CAD mass/inertia, frames, force application and acceleration calculation. Evaluate the magnetic map, force and back-EMF from each solver stage's pose, velocity and winding currents. Integrate those states together instead of holding back-EMF for a separate electrical advance. The existing split-step MuJoCo interface does not support its Runge–Kutta integrator, and changing only the mechanical integrator would leave the electrical coupling approximation in place.

[Embedded adaptive Runge–Kutta methods](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.integrate.solve_ivp.html) reuse stage calculations to estimate local error and retry unsuitable steps. They need no uniform allocation based on the requested final time. Their local tolerances still do not bound all accumulated trajectory errors. [CVODE's local-error control](https://sundials.readthedocs.io/en/latest/cvode/Mathematics_link.html#local-error-test) makes the same distinction. Specialized [global-error estimators](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.05166) propagate additional error information; our scalar accumulated work ledger does not implement that machinery. No claim of universal global accuracy follows from adopting a library solver.

The candidate integrates position, quaternion, body velocity, winding currents, command filters and work accumulators together. A separate standard adaptive solver integrates the passive Hall filter from that physical solver’s dense trajectory. Between hardware events this is one-way coupling: filter stages need field evaluations but no force-map recalculation. The composed dense solution supplies analogue ADC samples. Both solver errors must fit within the combined sensing allowance; separating the equations does not remove either error source. Overlapping scans retain their own startup response, obtained from the continuous filter solution by subtracting the exponentially decayed filter value at each scan start. This reproduces the existing gated-scan model without an exponential-current reconstruction. Physical feasibility of overlapping acquisition remains a hardware assumption. The legacy sensor path still uses the held-back-EMF reconstruction. Preserve ADC aperture/timing, noise and calibration, controller information separation and nominal current-based field subtraction. Numerical trial stages may evaluate deterministic physics, but cannot consume noise, deliver measurements or update hardware state. Known hardware events terminate smooth intervals; current extinction needs a located zero crossing and an explicit change of conduction equations. Coils reaching zero within the existing event-clock resolution are extinguished together; a terminal solver event must not leave another crossing channel conducting. Distinct crossing times remain separate, and a missed crossing beyond that clock allowance fails explicitly. Unsupported reconduction and contact still stop the airborne run. MuJoCo computes trial accelerations on separate mechanical data; rejected stages do not mutate accepted mechanics, drive state or sensor randomness. Body-frame angular velocity is converted to world coordinates for the quaternion derivative. Accepted quaternion normalization has an explicit drift limit. Both physical limits and accumulated energy defects can stop the candidate. Unsupported contact at a trial stage raises an error conservatively; this path does not qualify contact onset.

The candidate gate verifies exact free-fall and constant-coupling limits, nonlinear powered/switched coupling against independently refined references, arbitrary-orientation mechanics, Hall filtering and event/restart behavior. Compare tighter tolerances over bounded prescribed-input intervals and retain the combined field-error criterion. Measure actual magnetic evaluations and runtime on the shortest representative coupled case before extending its duration. The model owns the local relative tolerance and work scale. Absolute component scales derive conservatively from the existing position/orientation/velocity/current/field allowances and the solver state count, accounting for its RMS error norm. These are local solver scales, not an assertion that embedded errors equal the old full/half-step discrepancies. Accepted-step energy checks and external reference comparisons remain independent of local acceptance.

Keep energy/work accounting as an independent acceptance diagnostic with the existing total limits. A changed accounting formulation must be reconciled against the retained signed and absolute defects, not represented as the same metric. Do not loosen a limit, reset an accumulator or project the state onto an energy surface to make a run pass. The candidate sums absolute mechanical-work defects and absolute total electrical–mechanical balance defects over accepted steps and stops if either exceeds the existing total energy allowance. Work is integrated from stage power rather than frozen back-EMF, so the reference has improved and the ledger is not numerically identical to the legacy one. The old duration-proportional rule remains only in the existing paths pending the agreed adoption review. Energy counters survive restart; a requested longer duration does not tighten every candidate local step in advance. Joint checkpoints retain the original CAD mass-property record without unit conversions. Reconstruction uses that record before creating mechanics, magnetics or sensing, then enforces the original source, construction, scene and timing identity checks. Pending scans, continuous filter memory and both evaluation counters survive restart. The optional atlas backend is shared with the legacy adaptive implementation and reaches both controller allocation and temporary physical trial stages. Joint checkpoints record backend mode, cache and evaluator identity, require the matching cache and runtime explicitly, and reject older schemas or changed sources. The optional native spline runtime records its compiled source/binary and thread-library policy, evaluates independent point batches in their original order, and restores the reference evaluator when closed. Changed evaluator or worker identities cannot silently resume the same checkpoint. Direct fields remain the default. Cache field/force screening and bounded coupled comparisons are separate gates; neither replaces combined sensing-error accounting. Reproduction of older evidence uses its recorded source snapshot.

## Accuracy accounting

The [numerical-accuracy verifier](../numerical_accuracy.py) allocates a fraction of nominal scan-averaged analogue sensor noise to total deterministic field error. This is an engineering accuracy criterion, not a statistical guarantee or a claim that physical noise cancels numerical error. The allocation reuses the model's numerical fraction and actual scan correlation. Time integration, curved-conductor approximation, interpolation and sensor-history reconstruction must fit together within the total; each cannot spend the entire allowance independently.

For the fixed coil/sensor geometry, the maximum row sum of the absolute map difference, multiplied by the channel-current limit, bounds field disagreement for every allowed current combination. The verifier also evaluates the curved CAD wire directly using independent Biot–Savart quadrature, including the same ideal return route. Quadrature refinement establishes reference convergence empirically. The straight-filament production formula, CAD path approximation and reference integration are thereby checked separately. Positive sensor filtering and sample averaging cannot amplify a bounded additive coil-field error. Calibration/gain factors must be included when transporting this nominal-map bound into the full sensor model.

The combined sensing check evaluates actual CAD Hall fields along the manufactured coupling benchmark and its independent continuous reference. The joint candidate uses its dense physical/filter trajectory and the same scan-sampling implementation as the simulator. Magnet and coil filter errors are checked separately, then the directly integrated total filter is checked against their sum so cancellation cannot conceal error. The independent linear-filter reference supplies its exact sparse Jacobian to the stiff library solver; this changes computational work, not the filter equation or accuracy criteria. The legacy path retains its recorded-trajectory reconstruction check. Independently integrated sensor filters include power-up, staggered acquisition, sample averaging and current extinction. Per channel, the check sums absolute temporal/reconstruction discrepancies, the all-current-pattern conductor bound and reference-refinement discrepancies, including calibration gains. It does not allow magnet and coil errors to cancel. Analytical constant-input and clipped-decay responses check the filter reference. This discharges the combined budget for those bounded benchmark signals; it does not certify every future motion. The accelerated-backend check separately replays coupled direct and cached trajectories, refines direct integration, and independently filters each dense physical trajectory. It sums absolute magnet/coil differences between backends, direct temporal-refinement differences, production total-filter residual, conductor bounds and independent-filter disagreement. Calibration and offset response are included before comparison; ADC quantization and intentional noise remain physical sensing effects. This is an empirical comparison over its declared interval, not a uniform interpolation or global trajectory-error bound. Saved raw analogue samples and RK dense-output coefficients support diagnosis without recomputing forces; their numerical-library and source provenance must remain attached.

In the coil-cache application comparison, one complete sensor-feedback trajectory supplies recorded setpoint and applied-voltage histories to native-reference and tighter-integration physical replays. The replays evolve their own currents, back-EMF and mechanics; they do not overwrite physical states with the reference. Motion, current and raw Hall discrepancies are checked under these matched drive histories. Controller allocations are independently compared at the same estimated inputs, without changing the commands used by the feedback run. Each trajectory's production Hall samples are also compared with independent convolution of that trajectory's calibrated magnet and coil fields, including power-up and individual sample timing; quadrature refinement and direct analytical fields check the reference. This verifies filtering conditional on the supplied trajectory and complements spatial field qualification. The full feedback run separately retains observer, tracking, contact, operating and energy checks. Numerical agreement and a successful nominal movement do not establish disturbance recovery; that requires a separate feedback check. Independently evolving quantized controllers can produce different physical inputs, so their current disagreement is retained as feedback-sensitivity evidence rather than used alone to measure integration error. Earlier failed comparisons remain unchanged. Verification reads true states without supplying them to the controller.

Temporal error requires a different argument. A local step discrepancy starts both trial branches from the same approximate state; neither contains the accumulated difference from the exact solution. In general, the next global error is bounded by the propagated previous error plus the new local defect. Establishing a useful bound therefore requires both a defect estimate and control of error amplification over the supported motion and time horizon. The adaptive wrapper checks local state/field defects and accumulated mechanical-work defects; it does not propagate a global state-error bound. Neither a small local tolerance, energy consistency nor successful short trajectory comparisons closes that stronger obligation. The independent smooth and switched benchmarks quantify accumulated error over their declared intervals; they do not supply a continuous-domain amplification bound for arbitrary geometry and duration.

The fixed-voltage levitating equilibrium has physically unstable modes in the current model. The error-growth diagnostic reuses the magnetic stiffness calculation and includes winding resistance, inductance and motion-induced back-EMF. Derivative refinement, state rescaling and an independently reduced characteristic equation check the local growth rate. This describes amplification near equilibrium, not an actual long flight or a verdict on feedback stability. Long prescribed-input trajectory separation can therefore combine numerical defects with physical instability. An acceptance strategy must distinguish these mechanisms before using long open-loop agreement as a general simulator gate; existing failed comparisons are not reclassified as passes.

The verification strategy separates equation correctness, bounded numerical accuracy and control robustness. Independent force/flux, circuit, mechanics and energy references check the equations. Prescribed-input benchmarks compare the integrator with independently resolved solutions over a declared bounded interval, holding the magnetic equations fixed. They check both accumulated state error and energy balance; local acceptance alone is insufficient. Full-duration uncontrolled trajectory identity is not a universal prerequisite for a numerically verified implementation. A longer application still needs numerical uncertainty appropriate to its decisions, supported-domain guards and controller recovery checks. Neither physical instability nor successful control excuses a failed numerical budget, and energy balance alone cannot establish trajectory accuracy.

## General properties and existing checks

This is a mechanism map, not a list of every test or historical run. Test links identify existing implementations; current execution results and limitations remain in the verification record. A check is independent only to the extent that its reference avoids the implementation being checked.

| Property | Existing checks | What they do not yet establish |
|---|---|---|
| Correct mechanics, frames and force application | [Motion tests](../tests/test_motion.py): analytical acceleration, rotation/gyroscopic behavior, torque anchors, gravity ownership, CAD inertia, native-energy comparison and replay | Qualified contact dynamics and unaccounted external work |
| Correct winding response and switching | [Electrical tests](../tests/test_electrical.py): integrated circuit equation, interval-current impulse, independent quadrature of clipped decay and filter response, unsupported-state rejection | Real semiconductor switching and mutual inductance; independent flux and held-circuit energy references are supplied by the coupling check below |
| Correct magnetic force/torque and approximation interfaces | [Force tests](../tests/test_magnetic_forces.py): analytical uniform-field cases, rigid transforms, coil-column identity and parallel equivalence; [atlas tests](../tests/test_magnetic_atlas.py): direct comparisons, boundaries and cache integrity | A continuous-domain magnetic-error bound or independent verification of all nonuniform-field assumptions |
| Causal sensing and information separation | [Sensing tests](../tests/test_sensing.py): analytic filter responses, independent convolution, coil subtraction, scan/delivery timing and measurement-based estimation; combined sensing check above | Component realism and an all-motion global field-error bound |
| Reliable adaptive stepping and event handling | [Adaptive tests](../tests/test_adaptive_airborne.py), [physical-verification tests](../tests/test_physics_verification.py) and [airborne tests](../tests/test_airborne.py): analytical free-fall defect, accumulated energy budget, rollback, filter memory, clocks, shared domain guards and restart | A rigorous global state-error bound for arbitrary trajectories |
| Separation of numerical and control sensitivity | [Shared-input tests](../tests/test_airborne_inputs.py) and retained prescribed-input refinements hold voltage instructions and hardware schedules fixed | Controller stability, recovery from physical disturbances or universal agreement of rounded sensor codes |

The [coupling verifier](../coupling_verification.py) adds independent references rather than more flight scenarios. It integrates magnetic flux through a closed test winding and differentiates it under world translations and rotations about the piece centre of mass. This checks the force/torque map and therefore its back-EMF coefficients without reusing the Lorentz line integral. Surface quadrature and finite-difference refinement are checked; the permanent-magnet field library is still shared. The relation follows the flux/coenergy formulation in [MIT's electromechanical energy treatment](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/res-6-003-electromechanical-dynamics-spring-2009/c6b4266de588586b8d05d8613908fe91_chap03_emd.pdf).

The same verifier compares the production MuJoCo/electrical advance with a matrix-exponential solution of constant vertical coupling, including gravity. It independently integrates terminal work and resistive loss and compares them with changes in kinetic, gravitational and inductive energy. Separate held-back-EMF checks cover current extinction and signed energy returned through the ideal clamp. The smooth benchmark tests the expected first-order global behavior of the held-back-EMF coupling; it does not replace the physical magnetic map or prescribe a production time step. Variable geometry, rotation and changing back-EMF during extinction remain outside that reduced energy benchmark.

Its variable-coupling benchmark supplies an analytical flux with quadratic vertical dependence and cosine yaw dependence. Differentiating that flux supplies position-dependent force and yaw torque, with the corresponding motion-induced voltage. The production MuJoCo/RL advance and adaptive scheduler integrate those equations. Independent scaled ODE references use [SciPy's DOP853 and Radau methods](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.integrate.solve_ivp.html), with separate extinction-event detection and analytical ballistic motion afterwards. Reference-method agreement, energy balance and limiting analytical cases check the reference itself. Powered and switched cases share the existing motion/current and reduced energy allowances. Fixed temporal refinement separates integration error from adaptation; the batch stops at its first failed case.

This is a manufactured integration test with CAD mass, inertia and circuit parameters, not another magnetic backend or a controlled flight. Only vertical translation and principal-axis yaw are excited. Passive Hall fields are zero in this adapter; the separate combined sensing check evaluates CAD fields along its saved trajectories. The work accumulator participates in rejected-step rollback. After current extinction the reference also exposes the known mechanical-energy loss of semi-implicit free-fall stepping: it is proportional to mass, squared gravity and squared step duration. This gives a direct explanation for temporal drift without attributing it to sensor noise or feedback.

The numerical-accuracy verifier also checks curved force quadrature over the existing static verification poses and differentiates flux through a curved fan surface bounded by an actual CAD coil. This flux reference avoids the Lorentz force/torque formula while sharing the CAD path samples and permanent-magnet field library. Surface order and pose-derivative resolution are checked separately. Analytical circular-loop tests cover line integration, surface flux, force and COM torque. These checks establish sampled spatial accuracy and interface consistency; they do not bound accumulated trajectory error or prove the material/filament model against hardware.

## Finite verification completion checklist

The gate applies to a declared airborne model and intended use. Existing evidence should discharge requirements wherever its sources, settings and assumptions match. Complete the following items before resuming controller qualification; this is not a request for a new trajectory for every pose.

- **Declare the supported domain.** Record the geometry/material assumptions, permitted motion and electrical regimes, time horizon, magnetic backend and sensor model. Separate supported mechanisms from excluded ones. Map each applicable condition to an existing guard, add a missing guard where needed, or narrow the claim. A physical rating and an interpolation domain are different constraints.
- **Close the independent coupling checks.** Verify magnetic power transfer and electrical–mechanical energy accounting, including resistive losses and current extinction. Check back-EMF against an independent flux/work reference where applicable. Reusing a matrix and its transpose makes instantaneous power reciprocal by construction; that identity alone cannot detect an incorrect shared matrix or sign convention. Account for external work and dissipation rather than demanding conservation from a driven, lossy system.
- **Establish the temporal-accuracy argument.** Identify the coupled method's assumptions and expected convergence behavior. Use analytical or independently resolved smooth and switched coupling benchmarks to check the error estimator, accumulated state error and energy balance over their declared bounded horizons. Reuse existing switching/rollback evidence. Check temporal refinement independently of magnetic refinement. Explain and resolve failed budgets before qualification; do not assume local tolerance or energy balance alone guarantees global trajectory accuracy. Keep application-horizon accuracy and controller robustness separate from these implementation checks.
- **Allocate the numerical-error budget.** Relate acceptable numerical error to the intended motion, current and sensing decisions. Separate time integration, conductor discretization, atlas interpolation and sensor-history/filter reconstruction. Account conservatively for shared or systematic errors; do not assume independent random cancellation. Use the overall analogue-field criterion defined by the model. Keep measured discrepancies separate from limits, and retain existing limits unless a justified change is explicitly recorded.
- **Select and complete the bounded regression suite.** For each still-unverified mechanism, name the property, independent reference, acceptance rule and smallest discriminating test before launching it. Cover smooth coupling, switching/current extinction, numerical boundaries, causality and rollback. Add a geometry or trajectory only when it exposes a distinct unresolved mechanism or extends the declared domain. Do not run an exhaustive product of positions, tilts and movements.
- **Freeze the verification record.** Record the supported domain, assumptions, model/source/dependency identities, numerical settings, test evidence and unresolved exclusions in the existing evidence record. Every required property must have supporting evidence; a stopped process or a completed run is not sufficient. Qualification is conditional on that declared scope, not a claim that the physical chessboard has been validated.

The [earlier implementation record](../results/physics_verification/implementation_verification/summary.json) retains the independent flux/circuit references, sampled spatial checks and shared runtime guards. The [joint candidate record](../results/physics_verification/joint_passive_filter/qualification.json) supplies the affected temporal, passive-filter, event and checkpoint verification for the revised integration architecture, with explicit source provenance. Together they support bounded, direct-field, single-piece airborne investigation. Controlled-flight performance, application-horizon accuracy and physical validation remain separate; no universal simulation certificate is claimed. Current bounded controlled hover and local movement use the verified joint method; their outcomes are separate from this numerical gate.

## Changes and maintenance

A new movement within the qualified domain normally uses the same adaptive machinery and runtime guards; it does not automatically require another manual step-size campaign. Revisit affected verification when equations, coupling, numerical algorithms, geometry/material assumptions, hardware schedules or supported operating conditions change, or when evidence contradicts an accuracy claim. A tuned setting changes the configuration fingerprint and may require affected checks, even though this document stays unchanged.

Keep this document stable: update it when architecture, assumptions, verification obligations or test ownership change. Keep numerical values and pass/fail histories out. Maintain remaining tasks only in the plan and detailed evidence only in the verification record. Preserve failed evidence that still explains a limitation or regression, and remove superseded duplication. Physical validation and controller qualification remain separate from this numerical-verification gate.
